Hansi is a Chinese–English dictionary that runs entirely on your phone. No account, no ads, no tracking, and no internet permission: everything ships inside the app.

Full disclosure: Hansi is slopcoded. Most of its code was written together with an AI assistant (Claude), and its example sentences were generated by one. A human decided what to build, tested it and keeps it honest, but you should know how the sausage was made. The upside: it is libre software under the AGPL, built on well-known open dictionary data, and it is a genuinely good dictionary that costs nothing.

<b>Search the way you think</b>
• Type characters, pinyin or English in one search box; no mode switching
• Pinyin with or without tones: nihao, ni3hao3, nǐhǎo all work; v stands for ü
• Results ranked by how well they match and how common the word is, so "bank" gives 银行 first, not a list of rare characters
• Matching English words are shown in bold

<b>Learn the characters</b>
• Animated stroke order for every character
• Character decomposition and etymology: tap a character to see its parts
• Old HSK (2.0) levels 1–6 on common words
• Optional tone colours, several Chinese fonts, simplified or traditional

<b>See words in use</b>
• Example sentences at five difficulty levels for the most common words
• Tap any word in a sentence to look it up; reveal pinyin and English on demand
• Listen to words and sentences through your phone's text-to-speech

<b>Study with AnkiDroid</b>
• Send any word straight to AnkiDroid as a flashcard (hanzi, pinyin, English)
• Copy hanzi, pinyin, English or all three from any entry

<b>Text-to-speech needs a Chinese voice</b>
Hansi speaks through Android's text-to-speech system and brings no voice of its own. Most phones without Google services have no Chinese voice, so "Listen" stays silent until you install one. Free engines on F-Droid that offer Chinese include ChineseTtsTflite and SherpaTTS (download its Chinese language pack). Then select the engine under Settings → System → Languages → Text-to-speech output.
